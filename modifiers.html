Non-Destructive Modeling

Blender Modifiers

Modifiers let you smooth, mirror, thicken, deform, and duplicate 3D objects automatically — without permanently changing your base mesh.

Golden Rule 01

Stack Order Matters

Blender calculates modifiers from top to bottom. Drag Mirror above Subdivision Surface so the center seam welds cleanly.

Golden Rule 02

Apply Scale First (Ctrl + A)

If you scaled an object in Object Mode, press Ctrl+A → Scale before adding Bevel, Solidify, or Displace so widths stay uniform.

Golden Rule 03

Deform Needs Geometry

Deform modifiers (like Simple Deform, Displace, or Wave) move existing vertices — add loop cuts (Ctrl+R) or Subdivision first!

Ctrl + 1 / 2 / 3

Subdivision Surface

Divides each face into smaller faces to turn a blocky low-poly mesh into a smooth, curved surface.

  • Levels Viewport: Keep at 1 or 2 so your viewport stays fast.
  • Render: Set to 2 or 3 for smooth final renders.
💡 Pro Tip: Add support loops (Ctrl+R) near edges you want to keep sharp instead of cranking subdivision levels above 3.
Generate Beginner
Symmetry

Mirror

Automatically mirrors your mesh across the X, Y, or Z axis around the object's orange Origin dot — model half a character or car and get the other half free.

  • Axis (X / Y / Z): Choose which axis to mirror across (usually X).
  • Clipping: Turn this ON so center vertices stick together and don't pull apart.
💡 Common Fix: If both halves overlap in the same spot, your object's Origin dot is off-center — move the mesh in Edit Mode away from the orange dot.
Generate Beginner
Sharp Edges

Bevel

Rounds off razor-sharp 90° edges across your entire object so light catches the corners realistically.

  • Amount & Segments: Set how wide the bevel is and how many rounded cuts (2–3 segments) it uses.
  • Limit Method → Angle: Only bevels edges sharper than 30°.
💡 Pro Tip: Turn on Geometry → Harden Normals inside the Bevel modifier for clean, flat shading on hard-surface models.
Generate Beginner
Thickness

Solidify

Adds real 3D wall thickness to a flat plane or thin surface — essential for modeling cups, bowls, clothing, walls, and helmets.

  • Thickness: Controls how thick the wall becomes.
  • Even Thickness: Tick this checkbox to prevent corners from pinching.
💡 Pro Tip: Used in the classic Blender Donut tutorial to give the icing layer realistic thickness over the donut mesh.
Generate Intermediate
Cut & Join

Boolean

Cuts holes into a mesh or combines two overlapping objects using Difference (subtract), Union (merge), or Intersect.

  • Object: Use the eyedropper to pick the cutter mesh.
  • Solver (Fast vs. Exact): Switch to Fast if Exact leaves a hole or disappears.
💡 Common Fix: Hide the cutter object in the Viewport (H) after adding the Boolean so you can actually see the carved hole!
Generate Beginner
Repeat

Array

Creates repeating copies of an object in a row, grid, or circle — perfect for stairs, fences, chains, bricks, and pillars.

  • Count: Total number of repeated copies.
  • Relative Offset: Spacing between each copy along X, Y, or Z (e.g., 1.0 touches edge-to-edge).
💡 Pro Tip: Combine an Array modifier with a Curve modifier below it to make chains or tracks follow a curved path.
Deform Intermediate
Snap to Surface

Shrinkwrap

Wraps and sticks one mesh tightly onto the surface of another target object — used for decals, stickers, clothing, and retopology.

  • Target: Pick the base object you want to wrap onto.
  • Offset: Add a tiny offset (e.g., 0.002 m) so the wrapped mesh doesn't clip inside the target.
💡 Pro Tip: Place a Subdivision Surface modifier above Shrinkwrap so the mesh has enough vertices to hug the curved surface smoothly.
Deform Beginner
Twist & Bend

Simple Deform

Twists, bends, tapers, or stretches a mesh along an axis — great for twisted pillars, curved ramps, horns, and stylized props.

  • Mode: Choose between Twist, Bend, Taper, or Stretch.
  • Angle / Factor & Axis: Pick the deformation amount and axis (X, Y, or Z).
💡 Common Fix: A cube with only 8 vertices cannot bend smoothly — add loop cuts (Ctrl+R) first so the mesh has segments to bend!
Deform Intermediate
Follow Path

Curve

Bends a mesh along a Bézier or NURBS curve object — ideal for cables, pipes, roads, tentacles, and tank tracks.

  • Curve Object: Select the Curve path in your scene.
  • Deform Axis: Match the axis your mesh stretches along (usually X or Y).
💡 Common Fix: Keep both the mesh and the curve at the exact same Origin location (Alt+G) so the mesh doesn't fly off into space.
Deform Beginner
Terrain & Bumps

Displace

Pushes vertices outward or inward using a 2D heightmap or procedural texture (like Clouds or Voronoi) — perfect for mountains, rocks, and ripples.

  • Texture: Click New to create a height texture, then edit it in the Texture Properties tab.
  • Strength & Midlevel: Controls bump height (lower Strength to 0.1–0.3 for subtle surface detail).
💡 Common Fix: Displace only moves existing vertices — place a Subdivision Surface modifier (level 4–5) above Displace for smooth terrain!
Deform Intermediate
3D Cage Warp

Lattice

Deforms a detailed high-poly object using a simple low-resolution 3D cage (Shift+A → Lattice) — great for cartoon squash-and-stretch or reshaping complex props.

  • Object: Pick the Lattice cage object you placed around your mesh.
  • Lattice Resolution (U, V, W): Adjust cage subdivisions in the Lattice Data tab before editing it.
💡 Pro Tip: Scale the Lattice cage to fit your object in Object Mode first, then switch the Lattice to Edit Mode (Tab) to warp your mesh!
Deform Intermediate
Character Rigging

Armature

Binds a 3D mesh to a skeleton of bones so character limbs, creatures, and mechanical joints bend smoothly when animated in Pose Mode.

  • Object: Select your Armature rig (added automatically when parenting with Ctrl+P → With Automatic Weights).
  • Preserve Volume: Tick this checkbox to prevent elbows and knees from pinching when bent sharply.
💡 Pro Tip: Put the Armature modifier above Subdivision Surface in the stack so your viewport stays fast while animating!
Deform Beginner
Animated Ripples

Wave

Creates animated circular or linear wave ripples across a mesh automatically when you press Spacebar — ideal for water puddles, flags, and energy rings.

  • Motion (X / Y & Cyclic): Choose linear waves along one axis or circular ripples radiating from the center.
  • Height, Width & Narrowness: Control wave amplitude and spacing without setting keyframes.
💡 Pro Tip: Add a subdivided Plane (Shift+A → Plane, then subdivide in Edit Mode) and press Spacebar to see the wave animate immediately!
Normals 4.0+ New
Auto Smooth

Smooth by Angle

Replaces the old "Auto Smooth" checkbox in Blender 4.1+. Smooths curved surfaces while keeping sharp corners crisp based on an angle threshold.

  • Angle: Default 30° — faces meeting at a shallower angle look smooth; sharper edges stay crisp.
  • Ignore Sharpness: Overrides manually marked sharp edges if enabled.
💡 Quick Shortcut: In Object Mode, right-click your mesh and choose Shade Auto Smooth to add this modifier automatically.
Normals Intermediate
Clean Shading

Weighted Normal

Fixes weird dark shading gradients on beveled hard-surface models by weighting vertex normals toward larger flat faces.

  • Weighting Mode: Keep on Face Area so large flat panels stay perfectly flat in reflections.
  • Keep Sharp: Tick this option so sharp seams and bevel boundaries stay crisp.
💡 Pro Tip: Place Weighted Normal at the very bottom of your modifier stack (right below Bevel and Smooth by Angle).
